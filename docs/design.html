Snack Counter Automation

A chat bot that owns every refill from empty to reimbursed. It picks exactly one buyer, enforces the budget, files the HR claim, chases it, and sends management one report a month.

2offices · NOK & INR
~35volunteers
1buyer per refill, guaranteed
65automated tests

01 What "automate" means here

People still buy the snacks and HR still approves claims. The bot takes over the coordination, which is the part that fails today: who buys, how much they can spend, filing and chasing the claim, and reporting. It lives in the chat tool everyone already uses (slash commands and buttons), so there is no new app, login or habit.

02 How a refill moves from "empty" to "reimbursed"

  1. Report. Anyone types /snack empty. If a refill is already open, they're told who's on it. There's never a second one.
  2. Offer. The bot checks the budget, ranks who can refill today, and DMs the top person. 2 h to answer then the next person; after 3 offers it posts to the channel; after 4 h it alerts the admin. Anyone may press "Yes" at any time.
  3. Commit. The first "Yes" wins atomically. The buyer gets a spend limit (reserved against the budget) and a 24 h deadline. The channel is told "don't buy".
  4. Buy. /snack bought R-OSL-7 845.50 <receipt> chips, fruit. Within the limit, it goes straight to HR; over the limit, an admin approves it first.
  5. Claim. Amount, currency, description, receipt and employee ID go to HR automatically. The bot polls, retries outages, tells the buyer the outcome, and chases claims older than 10 days.
  6. Missed deadline. A reminder goes out 6 h before. After the deadline the refill is handed on. A late buyer may still report their purchase until someone else commits.

04 How each problem is prevented

Problem Box sits empty for days

A named person is asked directly instead of "someone". Silence counts as a "no" after 2 h, then it escalates to the channel and the admin. The report shows average hours the box was empty.

✓ tested: offer expiry, decline, broadcast and escalation

Problem Two people buy the same day

One active refill per office (atomic insert). Commit is a compare-and-set on a version number, so exactly one of N simultaneous "Yes" presses wins. A purchase is only accepted from the committed buyer, and anyone else who was in line is told "don't buy".

✓ tested: 5 simultaneous commits → exactly 1 winner

Problem A few people do most refills

Ranking: checked in at this office today → fewest refills in 30 days → longest since their last refill. The DM tells people why they were picked, and the report lists refills per person.

✓ tested: fairness, tie-breaks, 30-day window

Problem …and wait weeks for money

The claim is filed the moment the receipt is posted. The bot polls HR, retries outages, and chases claims older than 10 days. Anyone with 2 unreimbursed claims isn't asked until one clears.

✓ tested: HR outage retry, rejection, overdue chase

Problem Nobody knows the spend

/snack status live, plus a monthly report: spend vs budget per office in local currency, reimbursed / awaiting / rejected, and one converted total. Buyers tag items (chips, fruit) so the report shows what actually gets eaten.

✓ tested: NOK + INR totals, rounding, month boundaries

Context Hybrid work, holidays, visitors

Set once, not daily: usual office days (/snack days mon,wed,thu), trips (/snack visiting oslo 9 Nov–20 Nov), holidays. here / remote override a single day. A visitor is asked where they are, not at home; they buy in NOK, it's charged to Oslo, and HR pays them in INR.

✓ tested: weekly patterns, 2-week trips, overrides, holidays

05 Trade-offs and what I deliberately would not build

Trade-offs made

  • Ask one person first, not a free-for-all. Slower than broadcasting (up to 6 h of direct offers), but it creates ownership and fairness. Anyone can still step in.
  • Budgets kept in local currency. The budget check is exact and needs no exchange rate. FX is used only in the report, once per office total at a monthly rate.
  • Self-reported presence, not calendar or badge integration. One weekly pattern plus trips and holidays covers hybrid work with little effort. Anyone who never sets one counts as "maybe", and the 2 h timeout limits the cost of asking someone who is absent.
  • Over-limit receipts count as spent while in review. The money is already spent, and hiding it would flatter the budget.
  • HR "approved" = reimbursed; poll instead of webhooks. We can't see payroll, and polling works with any HR API.
  • Save first, then notify. Nobody is told something that didn't happen. A crash in between loses a message (an outbox table would fix it).

Deliberately not built

  • Stock or inventory tracking, sensors, snack voting
  • Receipt OCR; HR checks the image anyway
  • Company card or direct payments. It would remove the out-of-pocket problem, but that's a finance-policy change; recommended as a follow-up
  • A web dashboard
  • A rota or calendar sync; fair-share ranking replaces it
  • Working-day and public-holiday calendars
